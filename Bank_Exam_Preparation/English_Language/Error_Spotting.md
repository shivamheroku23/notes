# Error Spotting – Advanced Bible

---

## 1. Concept Map

- **Goal**: Find the part of the sentence that **breaks a grammar rule**.
- Most tested areas:
  - Subject–verb agreement
  - Tense consistency
  - Pronouns (he/she/it/they, who/whom)
  - Articles (a, an, the)
  - Prepositions (in, on, at, for, since, from, to)
  - Parallelism (same form in a list / pair)
  - Modifier errors (wrongly placed words/phrases)
  - Comparisons (than, as…as, between / among)
  - Conditional sentences (if + would / had / were etc.)
- Question formats:
  - Sentence split into **4–5 parts (A/B/C/D/E)** – choose the part with error.
  - Sentence with **underlined words/phrases** – choose the wrong one.
  - **No error** option often present.

---

## 2. Deep Logic – How to Think

### 2.1 Core Idea

Every English sentence must obey a **small set of rules**.  
Error spotting is simply:  

> Check each rule → if broken → that part has error.

We do not need to read like a story; we scan like a **grammar detective**.

### 2.2 Scan Order (Fixed Routine)

Always scan in this order (top-down):

1. **Subject–Verb Agreement**
   - Find subject.
   - Find main verb.
   - Are they matching in **number** (singular/plural) and sometimes **person**?
2. **Tense and Time Sense**
   - Is the tense correct for the time word (yesterday, now, since, for, always etc.)?
   - Is there **consistency** inside the sentence (no random tense jumps)?
3. **Pronouns**
   - Is the pronoun **clear** (who/what it refers to)?
   - Is it **correct in number/gender/case** (he/him, I/me, who/whom)?
4. **Articles and Determiners**
   - Is use of a / an / the / some / any / much / many / few / little correct?
5. **Prepositions**
   - Common fixed combinations: depend **on**, insist **on**, interested **in**, responsible **for** etc.
6. **Parallelism & Comparisons**
   - Items in a list or comparison should be in **same grammatical form**.
7. **Modifiers and Word Order**
   - Describing words/phrases should be next to what they describe.
8. **Miscellaneous but Important**
   - Double negatives (not + hardly / scarcely / never)
   - Redundancy (return back, more better)
   - Idiomatic usage (discuss about → wrong; discuss the issue → correct)

## 3. Algorithms / Templates

### 3.1 Universal Error-Spotting Algorithm

**Step 1 – Break the Sentence**

- Divide mentally into **meaningful chunks** (often already split as A/B/C/D).
- Read **slowly and clearly**.

**Step 2 – Subject–Verb Check**

- Ask: *What is the main subject?*  
- Ask: *What is the main verb linked to this subject?*
- If **subject is singular**, verb should normally be **verb + s/es** in simple present.
- If **subject is plural**, verb should be **base verb** in simple present.

> Template:  
> - Singular: The boy **plays**.  
> - Plural: The boys **play**.

**Step 3 – Tense Consistency**

- Note time words: **yesterday, last year, now, at present, always, since, for, by, before** etc.
- Check if all verbs fit the **same time frame**, unless there is a clear reason to change.

**Step 4 – Pronoun Check**

- Every pronoun must have a **clear noun**.
- Match **number** and **gender**.
- Case:
  - Subject: I, he, she, they, we, who
  - Object: me, him, her, them, us, whom
  - Possessive: my, his, her, their, our

**Step 5 – Articles / Determiners**

- Countability:
  - Countable singular → needs article/determiner: a book, the book, my book.
  - Uncountable → no “a/an”: information, advice, furniture.
- Specific vs general:
  - **The** for specific/known.
  - **A/An** for general/unknown, singular only.

**Step 6 – Prepositions and Fixed Phrases**

- Many verbs follow **fixed prepositions**. Memorize common ones.
- Check if preposition is:
  - Missing
  - Unnecessary
  - Wrong choice

**Step 7 – Structure / Parallelism**

- In lists joined by **and/or**, or after words like:
  - to, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also
- Ensure same form:
  - to eat, to sleep and to study (all “to + verb”) or
  - eating, sleeping and studying (all -ing forms).

**Step 8 – Modifiers and Misc.**

- Ensure introductory phrases describe the **right subject**.
- Remove double negatives and redundant words.

## 4. Level-wise Practice

### 4.1 Level 1 – Basic Pattern Familiarity

Identify the part with error. If no error, mark **No error**.

**Q1.**  
(A) Each of the students / (B) have submitted / (C) their assignments / (D) before the deadline. / (E) No error  

**Solution L1.1**

- Subject = “Each of the students” → grammatically **singular**.
- Verb used = “have” (plural).  
- Correct: “Each of the students **has** submitted their assignments before the deadline.”  
- Error in **part B**.

---

**Q2.**  
(A) She is living / (B) in Delhi / (C) since five years / (D) with her parents. / (E) No error  

**Solution L1.2**

- “Since” + point in time → present perfect / perfect continuous.  
- Here “since five years” is wrong; should be **for five years** or “since 2019” etc.
- Correct: “She has been living in Delhi **for five years** with her parents.”  
- Error in **part C**.

---

**Q3.**  
(A) He did not knew / (B) that his decision / (C) would affect / (D) so many people. / (E) No error  

**Solution L1.3**

- After “did not”, use **base verb** (know), not past (knew).
- Correct: “He did not **know** that his decision would affect so many people.”  
- Error in **part A**.

---

### 4.2 Level 2 – Mixed Grammar Rules

**Q4.**  
(A) The manager, as well as his assistants, / (B) were present / (C) at the meeting / (D) to discuss the new policy. / (E) No error  

**Solution L2.1**

- Rule: When subject is “A, as well as B” → verb agrees with **A** (first subject).
- Main subject: “The manager” (singular).
- Verb should be **was**, not “were”.
- Correct: “The manager, as well as his assistants, **was** present at the meeting…”  
- Error in **part B**.

---

**Q5.**  
(A) Neither the teacher / (B) nor the students / (C) was willing / (D) to cancel the class. / (E) No error  

**Solution L2.2**

- Rule: **Nearest subject** after “nor” decides the verb.
- Here “students” is plural → verb must be **were**.
- Correct: “Neither the teacher nor the students **were** willing to cancel the class.”  
- Error in **part C**.

---

**Q6.**  
(A) The principal discussed about / (B) the new rules / (C) with the teachers / (D) in the staff room. / (E) No error  

**Solution L2.3**

- Verb “discuss” is **not followed** by “about”.
- Correct: “The principal **discussed** the new rules with the teachers…”  
- Error in **part A**.

---

### 4.3 Level 3 – Exam-level Mixed Traps

**Q7.**  
(A) Hardly had the train left the station / (B) when we realized / (C) that some of our luggage / (D) were still lying on the platform. / (E) No error  

**Solution L3.1**

- After expressions like **hardly/scarcely/barely** in inverted form:
  - “Hardly had the train left… when …” → correct structure.
- Subject in part D = “some of our luggage” → luggage is **uncountable**, treated as singular.
- Verb should be **was**, not “were”.
- Correct: “…that some of our luggage **was** still lying on the platform.”  
- Error in **part D**.

---

**Q8.**  
(A) Being a rainy day, / (B) the match / (C) was postponed / (D) to the next week. / (E) No error  

**Solution L3.2**

- Intro phrase “Being a rainy day” seems to modify “the match”, but **match cannot be a day**.
- Better: “As it was a rainy day, the match was postponed…”  
- Error in **part A** (misplaced modifier).

---

**Q9.**  
(A) Many a student / (B) were absent / (C) from the class / (D) due to the festival. / (E) No error  

**Solution L3.3**

- “Many a + singular noun” takes **singular verb**.
- Correct: “Many a student **was** absent from the class…”  
- Error in **part B**.

---

**Q10.**  
(A) The performance of the players / (B) as well as the coach / (C) are being appreciated / (D) by the spectators. / (E) No error  

**Solution L3.4**

- Main subject: “The performance” (singular).
- “as well as the coach” is extra.
- Verb should be **is**, not “are”.
- Correct: “The performance of the players as well as the coach **is being appreciated**…”  
- Error in **part C**.

## 5. Common Traps and How to Avoid Them

### 5.1 Traps

1. **Nearest-noun confusion**
   - Verb agrees with **main subject**, not the nearest noun.
   - “The bouquet of flowers **is** on the table.” (Not “are”)

2. **Plural-looking singular nouns**
   - News, physics, mathematics, measles, mumps → usually **singular**.
   - “The news **is** surprising.”

3. **Either/neither/each/everyone**
   - Treated as **singular**.
   - “Each of the players **is** ready.”

4. **Data, criteria, media**
   - Traditionally plural (data are, criteria are), but in everyday usage often singular.
   - Bank exams may test classical rule: **data are**, **criteria are**.

5. **Since / For confusion**
   - Since + **point in time** (since Monday, since 2018).
   - For + **period** (for two days, for three years).

6. **Did + Verb**
   - After “did not / did”, always use **base verb**: did not go, did not know.

7. **Double negatives**
   - “Hardly… not”, “scarcely… no” → incorrect.
   - Use only one negative element.

8. **Wrong prepositions after common verbs**
   - discuss about, explain about, order for, request for (as verb) → wrong.
   - Correct: discuss the case, explain the rule, order tea, request leave.

9. **Redundant pairs**
   - return back, repeat again, more better, most unique → avoid.

10. **Comparison errors**
    - When comparing, make sure you compare **same kinds of things**.
    - “The population of Mumbai is greater than **that of** Delhi.” (Not “…than Delhi”)

## 6. Micro-Revision Summary

Use this as a quick 1–2 minute revision sheet.

1. **Always locate the main subject and main verb first.**  
   - Check number agreement. That alone catches many errors.

2. **Look for time words.**  
   - Match them with correct tense (since/for → perfect; yesterday → past; now → present).

3. **Check pronouns carefully.**  
   - Clear reference, correct case (I/me, who/whom), correct number/gender.

4. **Articles and determiners.**  
   - Singular countable noun must have a/an/the/my etc.  
   - Uncountable nouns do not take a/an.

5. **Prepositions = fixed friends of verbs.**  
   - Learn common pairs: insist on, depend on, responsible for, interested in, fond of.

6. **Parallel structure.**  
   - In lists and comparisons, keep same grammatical pattern.

7. **Avoid double negatives, redundancy and wrong idioms.**  
   - No “did not knew”, “hardly not”, “return back”, “discuss about”.

8. **Modifier check.**  
   - Intro phrases must clearly and logically match the subject that follows.

If you follow the **fixed scan order** on every question, your accuracy in error spotting will rise sharply and you will start recognizing repeated patterns used in bank exams.
