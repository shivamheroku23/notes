# Idioms and Phrases – Advanced Bible

---

## 1. Concept Map

- **Goal**: Understand and choose correct **idiomatic expressions** in context.
- Idiom = a group of words whose meaning is **not obvious** from individual words.
  - Example: “once in a blue moon” → very rarely.
- Phrase = small group of words acting as a unit in a sentence.
- Bank exam focus:
  - Meaning of idioms.
  - Correct usage in sentences.
  - Choosing the option that best replaces an idiom.

---

## 2. Deep Logic – How to Think About Idioms

### 2.1 Why Idioms Are Tricky

- Literal meaning is different from actual meaning.
  - “to kick the bucket” (literally kick a bucket) → to die.
- So, translation word-by-word does not work.
- You must **learn idiom as a single unit**, with meaning and sample sentence.

### 2.2 How Exams Test Idioms

1. Sentence given with idiom underlined; you must choose:
   - Meaning of the idiom.
2. Idiom given; you must pick **sentence where it is used correctly**.
3. In some papers, idioms may appear in **Cloze Test / RC** indirectly.

---

## 3. Learning Algorithm for Idioms

1. **Group by theme**
   - Work, money, time, success, failure, emotions.
2. **Use simple story or image in your mind**
   - For “spill the beans” imagine someone literally spilling beans and revealing secret.
3. **Always learn with an example sentence**
   - Idiom + Meaning + Own sentence.

---

## 4. Important Exam-style Idioms (with Examples)

Below are some high-frequency idioms.

1. **At the eleventh hour**  
   - Meaning: at the last possible moment.  
   - Example: He submitted his project at the eleventh hour.

2. **Once in a blue moon**  
   - Meaning: very rarely.  
   - Example: He calls his parents once in a blue moon.

3. **Take something with a pinch of salt**  
   - Meaning: not completely believe something; be cautious.  
   - Example: You should take his promises with a pinch of salt.

4. **Break the ice**  
   - Meaning: do or say something to make people feel more comfortable in a new situation.  
   - Example: The teacher cracked a joke to break the ice on the first day of class.

5. **Hit the nail on the head**  
   - Meaning: describe exactly what is causing a problem.  
   - Example: When she said that lack of planning was the issue, she hit the nail on the head.

6. **Burn the midnight oil**  
   - Meaning: study or work late into the night.  
   - Example: Students often burn the midnight oil before exams.

7. **Cut corners**  
   - Meaning: do something in a cheap or easy way that is not good or safe.  
   - Example: The builder cut corners, and the house started developing cracks.

8. **In hot water**  
   - Meaning: in trouble.  
   - Example: He was in hot water after missing an important meeting.

9. **Under the weather**  
   - Meaning: feeling slightly sick or unwell.  
   - Example: I am feeling a bit under the weather today.

10. **Spill the beans**  
    - Meaning: reveal a secret.  
    - Example: She spilled the beans about the surprise party.

## 5. Level-wise Practice

### 5.1 Level 1 – Simple Meaning Questions

**Q1.** What is the meaning of the idiom “once in a blue moon”?  

A) very often  
B) very rarely  
C) on a rainy day  
D) at night  

Answer: **B) very rarely**.

---

**Q2.** What is the meaning of “burn the midnight oil”?  

A) waste time  
B) sleep early  
C) work late into the night  
D) enjoy with friends  

Answer: **C) work late into the night**.

---

**Q3.** What does “in hot water” mean?  

A) in trouble  
B) in comfort  
C) on vacation  
D) in doubt  

Answer: **A) in trouble**.

---

### 5.2 Level 2 – Use in Sentence

Choose the option that best explains the idiom in the sentence.

**Q4.** “The teacher’s joke helped to break the ice at the beginning of the workshop.”  

A) cool the room  
B) make people feel more comfortable  
C) start a fight  
D) stop the program  

Correct meaning: made the atmosphere relaxed.  
Answer: **B**.

---

**Q5.** “The team was cutting corners to finish the project on time.”  

A) working very slowly  
B) increasing the budget  
C) doing things in a cheap and careless way  
D) taking extra care  

Answer: **C**.

---

**Q6.** “You should take his advice with a pinch of salt.”  

A) follow it blindly  
B) ignore it completely  
C) consider it carefully before believing  
D) agree without thinking  

Meaning: do not believe completely, be careful.  
Answer: **C**.

---

### 5.3 Level 3 – Exam-style Usage

**Q7.** Choose the sentence in which the idiom is used correctly: “under the weather”.  

A) He was under the weather while winning the race.  
B) She went under the weather to find her lost ring.  
C) I am feeling under the weather, so I will not come to office today.  
D) They drove under the weather to reach the station.  

Correct usage: for feeling unwell.  
Answer: **C**.

---

**Q8.** Choose the sentence that best expresses the meaning of “hit the nail on the head”.  

A) He hurt his hand while working with a hammer.  
B) He exactly identified the real problem.  
C) He repaired the chair successfully.  
D) He made a lot of mistakes while speaking.  

Answer: **B**.

---

**Q9.** What is the meaning of the idiom “at the eleventh hour” in the following sentence?

“He always pays his bills at the eleventh hour.”  

A) at the first opportunity  
B) at the last possible moment  
C) exactly on time  
D) very early in the morning  

Answer: **B**.

---

**Q10.** Choose the correct meaning of “spill the beans” used in the sentence below:

“Please do not spill the beans about our plan.”  

A) throw away the food  
B) reveal the secret  
C) cook the meal  
D) change the plan  

Answer: **B**.

## 6. Common Traps and How to Avoid Them

1. **Literal Meaning**
   - Do not interpret idiom word by word.
   - Learn idiomatic meaning as a whole chunk.

2. **Similar-sounding Idioms**
   - Example:
     - “hit the nail on the head” → identify exact problem
     - “hit the sack” → go to sleep
   - Do not mix them.

3. **Wrong Usage in Sentence**
   - An idiom may be used only in certain contexts.
   - Read full sentence to ensure it fits.

4. **Over-Memorising Without Examples**
   - Always connect idiom with example sentence; it sticks better.

---

## 7. Micro-Revision Summary

1. Idioms = fixed expressions with special meanings.
2. Learn each idiom as a **unit**:
   - Idiom → Meaning → Example.
3. In exam:
   - Read full sentence.
   - Replace idiom mentally with meaning.
   - Choose option that matches this meaning.
4. Start with a list of about **50–100 common idioms** used in bank exams and revise frequently.

With this systematic approach, Idioms and Phrases will become a **quick scoring area** in English section.
