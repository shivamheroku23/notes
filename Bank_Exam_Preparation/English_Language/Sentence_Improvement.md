# Sentence Improvement – Advanced Bible

---

## 1. Concept Map

- **Goal**: Replace a wrong / weak part of the sentence with **better and correct** wording.
- Typical pattern:
  - A part of the sentence is **underlined**.
  - Options:
    - A/B/C/D: different rewrites
    - One option is usually “No improvement” / “No correction required”.
- What is tested?
  - Grammar (tense, subject–verb, pronouns, articles, prepositions)
  - Idiomatic usage (natural-sounding English)
  - Word choice (appropriate word for context)
  - Sentence structure & clarity
  - Conciseness (removing unnecessary words)

---

## 2. Deep Logic – How to Think

### 2.1 Core Idea

Sentence Improvement is **Error Spotting + Better Expression**.

Steps:

1. Check if the given part is **grammatically correct**.
2. If correct, ask: *Is there an option that is grammatically correct **and also better style/clarity**?*
3. Compare options carefully:
   - Remove grammatical mistakes.
   - Avoid awkward, wordy, or confusing phrasing.
   - Pick the option that sounds **natural, precise and concise**.

### 2.2 When to Choose “No Improvement”

- When **no option** is better than the original in both:
  - Grammar
  - Natural expression
- Many students fear “No improvement” and avoid it. That is a trap.
- If sentence is already correct and smooth, choose “No improvement”.

---

## 3. Algorithms / Templates

### 3.1 Sentence Improvement Algorithm

**Step 1 – Read Full Sentence First**

- Do not jump to options.
- Understand the basic meaning.
- Note the underlined part.

**Step 2 – Quick Grammar Check of Underlined Part**

Check:
- Subject–verb agreement
- Tense
- Pronoun reference and case
- Prepositions
- Articles / determiners
- Parallelism
- Comparisons

If you find a **clear grammar error**, eliminate options that **repeat the same error**.

**Step 3 – Read Each Option in the Sentence**

- Replace underlined part with each option mentally.
- Read whole sentence in your mind.
- Reject options that:
  - Break grammar rules
  - Sound awkward or unnatural
  - Change intended meaning

**Step 4 – Shorter and Clearer is Usually Better**

If two options are grammatically correct and have the same meaning:
- Choose the one which is:
  - Shorter
  - Clearer
  - Less repetitive

**Step 5 – Decide on “No Improvement”**

- After checking all options, if original is **best**, choose “No improvement”.

## 4. Level-wise Practice

### 4.1 Level 1 – Basic Grammar & Style

**Q1.**  
He **did not knew** the correct procedure.  

A) did not know  
B) did not knows  
C) has not knew  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L1.1**

- After “did not”, verb must be base form: **know**.
- A) “did not know” is correct.
- Answer: **A**.

---

**Q2.**  
The teacher **is knowing** the answer to this question.  

A) knows  
B) knew  
C) has knowing  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L1.2**

- “Know” is a state verb; normally not used in continuous form.
- Correct: “The teacher **knows** the answer…”
- Answer: **A**.

---

**Q3.**  
He is senior **than** me by two years.  

A) than I  
B) to me  
C) from me  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L1.3**

- With “senior, junior, inferior, superior, prior” etc., use preposition **to**, not “than”.
- Correct: “He is senior **to me** by two years.”
- Answer: **B**.

---

### 4.2 Level 2 – Mixed Grammar + Idiom

**Q4.**  
He denied **to accept** the offer.  

A) denying to accept  
B) denied accepting  
C) denied accepting of  
D) denied to be accepting  

**Solution L2.1**

- Verb “deny” is normally followed by **verb + ing** or noun:
  - denied doing this, denied the charges.
- “denied to accept” is incorrect.
- Among options:
  - B) “denied accepting the offer” is correct and natural.
- Answer: **B**.

---

**Q5.**  
The company is determined **for improve** its services.  

A) to improve  
B) for improving  
C) in improving  
D) to be improving  

**Solution L2.2**

- Pattern: “determined **to + verb**”.
- Correct: “determined to improve”.
- Answer: **A**.

---

**Q6.**  
We discussed **about** the issue in detail.  

A) discussed on  
B) had discussed about  
C) discussed  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L2.3**

- “Discuss” does not take “about”.
- Correct: “We **discussed the issue** in detail.”
- Option C) “discussed” (without “about”) is correct.
- Answer: **C**.

---

### 4.3 Level 3 – Exam-Level Mixed Issues

**Q7.**  
Being a very hot day, we decided **to stay at home rather going** out.  

A) to stay at home instead of going  
B) staying at home rather than go  
C) to be staying at home rather than going  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L3.1**

- Correct parallel pattern: “instead of / rather than + verb + ing”.
- Option A) “to stay at home instead of going out”:
  - “stay” with “to stay” is fine.
  - “instead of going” is correct.
- Between A and B:
  - A is clearer and more natural.
- Answer: **A**.

---

**Q8.**  
Hardly **had we reached the station when** the train left.  

A) did we reached the station when  
B) had we reached the station than  
C) had we reached the station when  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L3.2**

- Standard structure: “Hardly had we reached the station when the train left.”
- Original sentence is already correct.
- Options A and B are grammatically wrong.
- C repeats original structure (without “the” before train in given form).
- If original is fully correct, choose “No improvement”.
- Answer: **D**.

---

**Q9.**  
The report is **too important to be ignored by we**.  

A) too important to be ignored by us  
B) so important to be ignored by us  
C) very important to be ignored by us  
D) too important so that we cannot ignore  

**Solution L3.3**

- Pronoun after preposition “by” should be **object form**: by us.
- “too important to be ignored by us” is correct.
- “so important to be ignored” is incomplete; needs “that”.
- “very important to be ignored” wrong structure.
- D is wordy and changes pattern.
- Answer: **A**.

---

**Q10.**  
If he **would have worked harder**, he would have passed the exam.  

A) had worked harder  
B) would work harder  
C) should have worked harder  
D) No improvement  

**Solution L3.4**

- Third conditional structure:
  - If + **had + past participle**, + would have + past participle
- Correct: “If he **had worked harder**, he would have passed…”.
- Answer: **A**.

## 5. Common Traps and How to Avoid Them

1. **Ignoring the Full Sentence**
   - Many students only read the underlined part and options.
   - Always read full sentence to catch meaning change.

2. **Choosing an Option Just Because It “Sounds Fancy”**
   - Fancy words can be wrong or awkward.
   - Choose correctness and clarity, not complexity.

3. **Fear of “No Improvement”**
   - Examiners know students avoid it.
   - If original is clearly correct and natural, pick “No improvement” with confidence.

4. **Missing Hidden Grammar Errors**
   - Sometimes underlined part looks okay, but tense or agreement is wrong considering the rest of the sentence.

5. **Overlooking Parallelism**
   - Look at structures like:
     - “to read, writing and to speak” → should be “to read, to write and to speak” or “reading, writing and speaking”.

6. **Wrong Prepositions and Idioms**
   - Common pitfalls: discuss about, request for (as verb), order for, comprise of, cope up with.
   - Correct: discuss the matter, request leave, order tea, comprise, cope with.

7. **Unnecessary Repetition**
   - “He returned back to home again” → too many words.
   - Improved: “He returned home.”

## 6. Micro-Revision Summary

Use this quick list just before the exam.

1. **Process**
   - Read whole sentence → check grammar in underlined part → test each option in the sentence → pick grammatically correct + clear + concise option → if original best → “No improvement”.

2. **Key Grammar Areas**
   - Subject–verb agreement
   - Tense and time words
   - Pronoun form and reference
   - Articles and determiners
   - Prepositions and fixed phrases
   - Parallel structures

3. **Improvement Principles**
   - Prefer:
     - Correct over incorrect
     - Clear over confusing
     - Simple over wordy
   - Do not be afraid of “No improvement”.

4. **Watch Out For**
   - Wrong prepositions (discuss about, order for)
   - Wrong comparisons (superior than → superior to)
   - Conditionals (If + had + V3; would have + V3)
   - Double negatives and redundant words

Practise regularly and apply this fixed thinking pattern; with time, you will start **seeing improvements instantly** and your accuracy will rise in Sentence Improvement questions.
