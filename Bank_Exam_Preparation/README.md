## 🧩 1. Exam Pattern Overview

Most bank exams follow a **two-stage written pattern** (plus Interview for Officer posts):

### 📘 Prelims (Objective)

| Section                | Questions | Marks | Typical Time |
|------------------------|----------:|------:|-------------:|
| English Language       | 30        | 30   | 20 minutes   |
| Quantitative Aptitude  | 35        | 35   | 20 minutes   |
| Reasoning Ability      | 35        | 35   | 20 minutes   |
| **Total**              | **100**   | **100** | **60 minutes** |

- **Sectional timing** is common in SBI/IBPS – you **cannot switch** before the time is over.
- **Negative marking**: −0.25 for each wrong answer (usually).

### 📙 Mains (Objective – plus Descriptive in some exams)

Pattern slightly varies across exams, but broadly:

| Section                    | Questions | Marks | Time (approx) |
|----------------------------|----------:|------:|--------------:|
| Reasoning &amp; Computer Aptitude | 40–50     | 50–60 | 40–60 minutes |
| Data Analysis &amp; Interpretation | 30–35 | 50–60 | 35–45 minutes |
| English Language           | 35–40     | 40–50 | 35–40 minutes |
| General / Banking Awareness| 40–50     | 40–50 | 35–40 minutes |

For PO exams (SBI/IBPS), there is also:

- **Descriptive Test**: Letter + Essay (usually 25 marks, 30 minutes).

## 🎯 2. Section-wise Strategy Snapshot

### 🔢 Quantitative Aptitude (QA)

- Target is **high accuracy with selective attempts**.
- Focus areas:
  - **Simplification/Approximation**
  - **Number Series**
  - **Quadratic Equations**
  - **Arithmetic** (Percentage, SI/CI, Profit &amp; Loss, Ratio, Average, TSD, Time &amp; Work, etc.)
  - **Data Interpretation** in Mains
- **Golden rule**: *Do easy, familiar questions first; leave ego aside on tough ones.*

---

### 🧠 Reasoning Ability

- Highly **scoring** once concepts are clear.
- Focus areas:
  - Puzzles &amp; Seating Arrangement
  - Inequalities, Syllogism
  - Coding-Decoding
  - Blood Relation, Direction Sense
  - Input-Output, Data Sufficiency (Mains)
- **Key skill**: Visualisation + stepwise diagramming.

---

### 📚 English Language

- NOT about “big vocabulary”; it’s about:
  - **Basic grammar**
  - **Comprehension skills**
  - **Context-based vocabulary**
- Focus areas:
  - Error Spotting, Sentence Improvement
  - Cloze Test, Fill in the Blanks
  - Reading Comprehension
  - Para Jumbles
  - Vocabulary (Synonyms, Antonyms, Idioms)

---

### 🌏 General Awareness (GA)

- Most **ignored** but **highly scoring** in Mains.
- Focus:
  - **Banking Awareness**
  - **Current Affairs (last 4–6 months)**
  - **Static GK**: Countries–capitals, important days, national parks, dams, etc.
- Study from **reliable monthly PDFs** + revision notes.

---

### 💻 Computer Knowledge

- Usually asked at **basic level**:
  - Fundamentals of Computer
  - MS Office
  - Internet &amp; Networking basics
  - Common abbreviations &amp; terms
- Easy to score with **short crisp notes**.

## ⏱️ 3. Time Management Tips

### ⌚ During Preparation

- Study in **focused blocks of 45–60 minutes**, then break for 5–10 minutes.
- Use the **80/20 rule**:
  - 20% topics (like Arithmetic basics, DI, Puzzles, RC) give 80% of marks.
- Maintain separate **error log**:
  - Write down questions you got wrong + reason (concept / calculation / silly mistake).

### ⌛ During Exam

**Prelims Strategy (Per section of 20 minutes):**

- First **2–3 minutes**: 
  - Scan through and mark very easy questions (one-liners).
- Next **12–14 minutes**:
  - Attempt easy &amp; moderate questions only.
- Last **3–5 minutes**:
  - Attempt a few slightly tougher questions *only if* you are 80–90% sure.

**Target Attempts (with good accuracy):**

| Section   | Safe Attempts (Prelims) |
|----------|-------------------------|
| English  | 20–25                   |
| Quant    | 20–25                   |
| Reasoning| 20–25                   |

> [!TIP] ⚡
> It’s better to attempt a bit fewer questions with **high accuracy** than many questions with random guesses.

## 📅 4. Recommended 12-Week Study Plan

Assuming you’re preparing alongside college/job (3–4 hours daily):

### 🕓 Weekly Time Allocation

- **Quantitative Aptitude**: 1.5 hours/day
- **Reasoning**: 1 hour/day
- **English**: 45 minutes/day
- **GA + Computer**: 30–45 minutes/day (from Week 3)
- **Mock Tests**: 1–2 full mocks on weekends

---

### 📆 Weeks 1–4: Foundation Phase

- Build **conceptual clarity**:
  - Quant: Basics of Percentage, Ratio, Average, Simplification, Number Series.
  - Reasoning: Basic puzzles, seating, inequalities, syllogism.
  - English: Grammar basics, error spotting, short RCs.
- Start GA lightly (headlines only) from Week 3.

**Daily plan example (3.5 hours):**

```text
Quant           : 75 minutes
Reasoning       : 60 minutes
English         : 45 minutes
GA/Computer     : 30 minutes
Revision        : 15 minutes
```

---

### 📆 Weeks 5–8: Practice + Speed Phase

- Start **timed practice sets**.
- Introduce **Mains-level DI and puzzles**.
- English: longer Reading Comprehensions, Cloze tests, Para Jumbles.
- GA: Daily current affairs + weekly revision.

> [!NOTE] 🧾  
> Start taking **sectional tests** (Quant/Reasoning/English) 2–3 times a week.

---

### 📆 Weeks 9–12: Exam Simulation Phase

- 1–2 **full-length mock tests** every week (Prelims pattern).
- Detailed **analysis** after every mock:
  - Which questions took too much time?
  - Which topics are weak? Revisit notes.
- For Mains aspirants: begin solving **Mains-level mock** once a week from Week 10.
